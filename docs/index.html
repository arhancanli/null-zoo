The Null Zoo: Size and Power of Backtest-Overfitting and Data-Snooping Tests on Synthetic Searches with Known Ground Truth

Arhan Canli · Canli Capital · 5 October 2026 · Preprint, version 2.0.0 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23018987 (all versions)
PDFZenodoCode and dataPre-registration

Abstract

Corrections for backtest overfitting and data snooping—the deflated Sharpe ratio, the multiple-testing haircut, family-wise adjustments, White's Reality Check and Hansen's test for superior predictive ability (SPA)—are derived under assumptions that trading research violates. The Null Zoo is an open, versioned, pre-registered simulation benchmark that draws research searches (20 strategies, 504 daily returns) from nine return families with known ground truth and scores 14 validators and an oracle by size and power, with 10,000 searches per cell. With a stationary bootstrap of mean block length 8, Hansen's studentized SPA test and the stepwise SPA test built on it rejected more than 6% of skill-less searches at a nominal 5% in at least seven of nine families in each of two runs (up to 12.6%); the Reality Check exceeded 6% only under autocorrelation. The bootstrap holds fixed a noisy, downward-biased long-run variance, and negative skew makes a mean and its standard deviation dependent. Re-studentizing every resample, as Romano and Wolf recommend, brought the size to about the Reality Check's (5.0–6.2% outside the autocorrelated family). The excess reaches 12.9% (17.8% under negative skew) at the default block length of the Python package arch, whose SPA test does not studentize; at that block length even the Reality Check is liberal. A lag-one autocorrelation of 0.2 raises the size of single-series tests that ignore it to about 19.5%, and the deflated Sharpe ratio's 0.95 rule misses 90.8% of i.i.d. searches containing a strategy with a true Sharpe ratio of 2. On 51 two-year windows of 20 moving-average rules on US stocks since 1927, the tests disagree in 5. Of 16 pre-registered predictions, 13 held.
