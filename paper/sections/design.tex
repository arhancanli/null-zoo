\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Aims}
The study estimates, for each validator and each return family, (i) the probability that the validator calls the best of $N$ skill-less strategies skilled (size), and (ii) the probability that it rejects when one of the $N$ strategies has real skill (power). A validator is \emph{calibrated} in a family when its size is within Monte Carlo error of its nominal level.

\subsection{Data-generating mechanisms}
One replication draws a \emph{search}: $N=\nzTrials$ return series of $T=504$ periods, read as two years of daily returns ($q=252$ periods per year). Every series has population mean zero and population variance one, so every strategy's true Sharpe ratio is zero. In the power arm, series 0 receives a constant drift of $2/\sqrt{252}$ per period, giving it a true annualized Sharpe ratio of 2; the other series are unchanged. The eight families are:

\begin{description}
  \item[IID normal.] Independent standard normal returns; the case every correction is derived for.
  \item[Student $t_4$.] Student $t$ with 4 degrees of freedom, scaled to unit variance: symmetric fat tails. Its fourth moment is infinite, so sample kurtosis does not converge.
  \item[Skew $\pm1.32$.] A centred, unit-variance lognormal with log-standard deviation 0.4, whose skewness is $+1.32$, and its mirror image ($-1.32$).
  \item[GARCH(1,1).] $h_t = 0.05 + 0.10\,\varepsilon_{t-1}^2 + 0.85\,h_{t-1}$, $\varepsilon_t=\sqrt{h_t}\,z_t$ \citep{bollerslev1986garch}, with unconditional variance one; each series starts at $h=1$, $\varepsilon=0$, without burn-in.
  \item[AR(1).] $x_t = 0.2\,x_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-0.2^2}\,z_t$, started from its stationary distribution.
  \item[Two volatility regimes.] Normal returns whose standard deviation is 0.50 or 1.32 (0.6 and 1.6 rescaled to unit variance), switching with probability 0.02 each period.
  \item[Correlated trials.] $x_{k,t} = \sqrt{1/2}\,f_t + \sqrt{1/2}\,z_{k,t}$ with a factor $f_t$ common to all strategies, so every pair of strategies has correlation 0.5 and the search contains fewer than \nzTrials\ effectively independent trials.
\end{description}

Normal variates come from the Box--Muller transform \citep{box1958note}. The published run uses a 32-bit xorshift generator \citep{marsaglia2003xorshift}; the robustness run in Section~\ref{sec:robust} uses xoshiro128** \citep{blackman2021scrambled}. Instrument tests run before any validator is scored. On samples of 50,000 observations, every family's mean is within 0.03 of zero and its variance within 0.12 of one; the skewed families have skewness beyond $\pm1$, the $t_4$ family kurtosis above 5, the AR(1) family a lag-one autocorrelation within 0.02 of 0.2, the GARCH and regime families positive autocorrelation in squared returns, and the correlated family a pairwise correlation within 0.03 of 0.5. In three representative families (i.i.d.\ normal, negative skew and correlated trials) the drift gives series 0, and only series 0, an annualized Sharpe ratio within 0.2 of 2. Every cell reproduces from its seed.

\subsection{Estimands}
In each replication the validator sees all \nzTrials\ series, selects the one with the highest sample Sharpe ratio, and returns a $p$-value for the hypothesis that this best strategy has no skill. The estimands are the rejection rates $\Pr(p \le 0.05)$ under the null arm (size) and the skill arm (power). Because raw power rewards a test for being liberal, we also report size-adjusted power: the power at the cutoff at which the same validator rejects at most 5\% of the null arm in the same family. Finally, for the skill arm, we report the rate at which the validator rejects \emph{and} the selected strategy is the truly skilled one, since a rejection that crowns the wrong strategy is a discovery only in name.

\subsection{Methods}
Let $\widehat{SR}$ be the best strategy's per-period Sharpe ratio (sample standard deviation with divisor $T-1$), $\widehat{SR}_a=\widehat{SR}\sqrt{q}$ its annualized value, and $\hat\gamma_3,\hat\gamma_4$ its sample skewness and (non-excess) kurtosis. The validators are:

\begin{description}
  \item[LET.] The luck-equivalent-trials test. Under the null and normal returns, $t=\widehat{SR}\sqrt{T}$ is exactly Student $t$ with $T-1$ degrees of freedom, so one skill-less strategy reaches the observed value with probability $p_1=\Pr(t_{T-1}\ge t)$. The best of $N$ independent ones does so with probability $1-(1-p_1)^N$ \citep{sidak1967}, the reported $p$-value. As a test it is therefore the \v{S}id\'ak-adjusted Student $t$ test; the statistic reports the same evidence to users in units of trials, $N_q=\ln(1-q)/\ln(1-p_1)$.
  \item[LET-Lo.] LET after deflating $\widehat{SR}_a$ by the autocorrelation-adjusted annualisation factor of \citet{lo2002sharpe}, $\big[1+\tfrac{2\hat\rho}{1-\hat\rho}\big(1-\tfrac{1-\hat\rho^{\,q}}{q(1-\hat\rho)}\big)\big]^{-1/2}$, evaluated at the best series' lag-one autocorrelation $\hat\rho$ (clamped to $[-0.95,0.95]$), as the haircut code of \citet{harvey2015backtesting} does. The factor assumes geometric decay of the autocorrelations, $\rho_k=\rho^k$, which the AR(1) family satisfies exactly. With $q=252$ it is close to $\sqrt{(1-\hat\rho)/(1+\hat\rho)}$, the AR(1) long-run-variance correction.
  \item[LET-NN.] LET with the normal approximation and the non-normal standard error of \citet{mertens2002comments} evaluated at the estimate: $z=\widehat{SR}\sqrt{T-1}\,/\sqrt{1-\hat\gamma_3\widehat{SR}+\tfrac{\hat\gamma_4-1}{4}\widehat{SR}^2}$.
  \item[HC, HC-Lo.] The haircut Sharpe ratio of \citet{harvey2015backtesting}: the two-sided Student $t$ $p$-value of the best strategy, adjusted by Bonferroni for \nzTrials\ tests, without and with the Lo factor above. The Holm variant differs only when a strategy with a large \emph{negative} Sharpe ratio has the smallest two-sided $p$-value; in the robustness run it changed the decision in at most \nzHolmBonfMaxDiff\ of \nzRepsExt\ searches in any cell, so we do not tabulate it.
  \item[DSR.] The deflated Sharpe ratio \citep{bailey2014dsr}, the probabilistic Sharpe ratio \citep{bailey2012frontier} evaluated against a benchmark equal to the expected maximum Sharpe ratio of $N$ skill-less trials, computed with the best strategy's skewness and kurtosis, $N=\nzTrials$ and the cross-trial standard deviation of the \nzTrials\ annualized Sharpe ratios. \citet{bailey2014dsr} accept a strategy when $\mathrm{DSR}\ge 0.95$; we score $1-\mathrm{DSR}$ as its $p$-value, so that rejection at 5\% is exactly that rule.
  \item[Boot.] An i.i.d.\ bootstrap \citep{efron1979bootstrap} of the best strategy's demeaned returns. With $B$ resamples, the single-trial $p$-value is $(h+1)/(B+1)$, where $h$ counts resampled Sharpe ratios at or above the observed one, and it is then adjusted by \v{S}id\'ak for \nzTrials\ trials. The published run uses $B=400$. The smallest attainable adjusted $p$-value is then $1-(400/401)^{\nzTrials}=\nzBootMinPFourHundred$, so the test can reject only when no resample reaches the observed Sharpe ratio, which costs power without affecting validity. The robustness run uses $B=\nzBootDraws$, whose smallest attainable $p$-value is \nzBootMinPExt.
\end{description}

LET, HC, HC-Lo and DSR call the implementations behind the author's public calculator and API. LET-Lo applies the same implementation to a Lo-deflated Sharpe ratio. LET-NN, Boot and the autocorrelation estimate are written in the benchmark itself. No validator is tuned to the benchmark.

\subsection{Performance measures and Monte Carlo error}
The published run (v0) uses \nzRepsVzero\ replications per cell and fixed seeds (one per cell, recorded in the output). The Monte Carlo standard error of a rejection rate $\hat p$ from $n$ replications is $\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$: \nzSeFiveVzero\ percentage points at 5\% for $n=\text{\nzRepsVzero}$ and \nzSeFiveExt\ for $n=\text{\nzRepsExt}$. Every rate's standard error follows from this formula; the tables state $n$. We flag a size as miscalibrated when it lies outside the 99\% band around 5\%, which is \nzBandVzeroLo--\nzBandVzeroHi\% in the published run and \nzBandExtLo--\nzBandExtHi\% in the robustness run. With seven validators in eight families, a 99\% band keeps the expected number of false flags among the 56 size cells below one.
