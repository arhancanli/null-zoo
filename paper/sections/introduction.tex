\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A researcher who tries many variants of a trading strategy and reports the best one reports a biased number. The best of $N$ strategies with no skill has an in-sample Sharpe ratio that grows with $N$, roughly in proportion to $\sqrt{2\ln N}$ times the spread of the individual estimates \citep{bailey2014pseudo}, and the same selection effect pervades published factor research \citep{harvey2016cross}. Two families of corrections are now standard. The first adjusts the best strategy's own statistic: the deflated Sharpe ratio \citep{bailey2014dsr}, built on the probabilistic Sharpe ratio \citep{bailey2012frontier}; the multiple-testing haircut of \citet{harvey2015backtesting}; and family-wise adjustments such as those of \citet{holm1979} and \citet{sidak1967}. The second resamples the whole search: White's Reality Check \citep{white2000reality}, applied to trading rules by \citet{sullivan1999datasnooping}, Hansen's test for superior predictive ability \citep{hansen2005spa} and the stepwise procedures of \citet{romano2005stepwise} and \citet{hsu2010stepwise}.

Each correction rests on assumptions---normal or independent returns, independent trials, large samples---that real searches violate in predictable ways. Returns are fat-tailed, skewed, volatility-clustered and sometimes autocorrelated; strategy variants share most of their positions and so are correlated; and two years of daily data is a short sample for a variance estimate. Inference for a single Sharpe ratio under such violations is well studied \citep{lo2002sharpe,opdyke2007comparing,ledoit2008robust}, and simulations have assessed multiple-testing corrections for the cross-section of factors and anomalies \citep{harvey2020false,chordia2020anomalies}. We are not aware of a tabulation of how much each violation distorts each \emph{selection} correction when it is applied, as practitioners apply it, to the best of one strategy search.

This paper provides one with a simulation benchmark, the \emph{Null Zoo}. It draws complete research searches---\nz{trials} candidate strategies of \nz{obs} daily returns each---from nine return families whose ground truth is known by construction, applies \nz{v1.validator_count_no_oracle} validators and an oracle as their implementations are meant to be called, and records how often each declares a skilled strategy when there is none (its \emph{size}) and when one strategy has a true annualized Sharpe ratio of 1, 2 or 3 (its \emph{power}). The main run has \nz{reps.v1} searches per cell. Every governed run was pre-registered before it was run, following recent arguments that comparative simulation studies need the same protection against selective reporting as empirical ones \citep{pawel2024pitfalls,siepe2024simulation}. A surprising result was confirmed on fresh seeds under a new pre-registration before it was used; its mechanism, suggested by an unregistered pilot, was confirmed in a registered run of a second implementation in another language; and a further registered run maps where the remedies work and applies the tests to real data.

The findings, with a stationary bootstrap of mean block length $\mathrm{round}(T^{1/3})=8$ unless stated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Hansen's studentized SPA test, and the stepwise SPA test built on it, are oversized at this horizon; White's Reality Check is close to its level except under autocorrelation.} At a nominal 5\%, the consistent SPA test rejected more than 6\% of skill-less searches in \nz{v1.fails6.spa_consistent} of the 9 families in one run and in \nz{v1b.fails6.spa_consistent} in its confirmation, up to \nz{both.size_max.spa_consistent}\%; the Reality Check rejected \nz{both.size_range_nonar.reality_check}\% outside the autocorrelated family and \nz{v1.size.reality_check.ar1}--\nz{v1b.size.reality_check.ar1}\% within it.
  \item \textbf{The cause is a variance estimate the bootstrap holds fixed.} The statistic divides each mean by a long-run standard deviation estimated once and reused in every resample. That estimate is noisy and, computed from demeaned data, biased downward; a first-order account of both predicts \nz{approx.share.b8}\% and \nz{approx.share.b22}\% of the excess at block lengths 8 and 22. Under negative skew a mean and its estimated standard deviation are also dependent. \citet{romano2005stepwise} note that holding the estimate fixed is only first-order accurate and recommend re-estimating it in every resample; doing so with the resample's standard deviation brought the size to about the Reality Check's (\nz{v2.A.size_range_nonar.spa_c_boot_t}\% outside the autocorrelated family). The excess shrinks with the sample, grows with the number of strategies and the block length, and reaches \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_b22.iid_normal}\% (\nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_b22.skew_negative}\% under negative skew) at the default block length of the Python package arch; with the i.i.d.\ resampling of Hansen's own simulations it nearly disappears.
  \item \textbf{Every block bootstrap inherits the long-run variance estimate, and long blocks make every test liberal.} The bootstrap's conditional variance of a mean is the same estimate, so even the unstudentized Reality Check is affected: with one strategy its size rose from \nz{v2b.E.size.rc.k1.b1}\% at block length 1 to \nz{v2b.E.size.rc.k1.b22}\% at 22. At block length 8 the Reality Check and the bootstrap-$t$ stayed within Monte Carlo error for 1 to 100 strategies; at block length 22 both were liberal with up to 20. Re-studentizing by a natural block variance cured the bootstrap-$t$'s excess under autocorrelation (\nz{v2b.F.size.spa_c_boot_t_ge.ar1}\% to \nz{v2b.F.size.spa_c_nb_ge.ar1}\%) but was conservative elsewhere (\nz{v2b.F.size_range_nonar.spa_c_nb_ge}\%).
  \item \textbf{arch's released SPA test does not studentize, and the change merged to fix it makes it the fixed-variance test.} In arch \nz{v2.D.arch} \citep{sheppard2025arch} the \texttt{studentize} option has no effect. The test is far less oversized than the fixed-variance one, but rejects \nz{v2.A.size_range_nonar.spa_c_unstud_b22}\% outside the autocorrelated family at its default block length. A change merged on 6 October 2026 \citep{arch871}, not yet released when we checked, divides by the fixed standard error whenever \texttt{studentize} is true, its default. Run on \nz{archm.searches} null searches per family, the merged code rejected \nz{archm.size.arch_default_consistent.iid_normal}\% of i.i.d.\ normal and \nz{archm.size.arch_default_consistent.skew_negative}\% of negatively skewed searches at its default block length.
  \item \textbf{Studentizing and recentring pay in the cases they were designed for.} With volatilities from 0.5 to 2, studentizing raised power from \nz{v2b.C2.power.rc.low}\% to \nz{v2b.C2.power.spa_c_boot_t_ge.low}\% when the skilled strategy was the least volatile, but cost power when it was the most volatile (\nz{v2b.C2.power.spa_c_boot_t_ge.high}\% against \nz{v2b.C2.power.rc.high}\%), and the gain came from ranking by $t$-statistic, which a test without resampling does as well. When most strategies are clearly poor, consistent recentring raised power at the nominal level from \nz{v2b.C2.power.rc.poor}\% to \nz{v2b.C2.power.spa_c_ge.poor}\%. A floored statistic with a strict inequality can reject when no strategy beats the benchmark; counting ties, found in an exploratory check and then registered, brought that design's size from \nz{v2b.C2.size.spa_c_strict.poor}\% to \nz{v2b.C2.size.spa_c_ge.poor}\%.
  \item \textbf{Autocorrelation breaks the single-series tests that ignore it.} A lag-one autocorrelation of 0.2 raises their false-positive rate to about \nz{v1.size.luck_trials.ar1}\%; Lo's factor removes the excess in that case, its best, and kernel and block-bootstrap remedies reduce it.
  \item \textbf{The deflated Sharpe ratio with its authors' 0.95 rule tests a harder hypothesis than ``no skill''.} It fails to accept \nz{v1.dsr_fail_to_accept.iid_normal}\% of i.i.d.\ searches containing a strategy with a true Sharpe ratio of 2 over two years; of its \nz{v1.dsr_gap.total}-point shortfall against the \v{S}id\'ak test, \nz{v1.dsr_gap.threshold} points come from the threshold and \nz{v1.dsr_gap.statistic} from the statistic.
  \item \textbf{Selection, not correction, sets the ceiling.} An oracle that knows which strategy is skilled rejects in \nz{v1.power2.oracle_hac_t.iid_normal}\% of such searches; the best calibrated best-of-\nz{trials} tests reach about \nz{v1.power2.luck_trials.iid_normal}\%.
  \item \textbf{On real data the choice matters at the margin.} Applied to \nz{emp.rules} moving-average timing rules in \nz{emp.windows} two-year windows of US market data since 1927, the tests disagree in \nz{emp.split_windows} windows, and the tests found oversized reject most often.
\end{enumerate}
The mechanism in finding 2---a noisy long-run variance inflating a studentized test---is familiar from heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-robust inference \citep{kiefer2005new,sun2008optimal,lazarus2018har} and from tests of equal forecast accuracy \citep{diebold1995comparing,harvey1997testing,coroneo2020comparing}, and Romano and Wolf state the remedy for data-snooping tests. What the benchmark adds is the size of the effect where strategy research operates, its interaction with the maximum over strategies, with skewness and with the block length, evidence for Romano and Wolf's remedy and for its limits under serial dependence, and its consequences for widely used code.

The benchmark scores a statistic introduced by its own author, the \emph{luck-equivalent-trials} count, and the v1 and v1b runs chose the default tests of validation software published by the author's firm: the pre-registered rules chose the Reality Check for searches and LET-Lo, the author's statistic with Lo's factor, for single series. \citet{bartos2025living} argue that simulation studies designed by a method's developers carry a conflict of interest. We report that conflict (see Competing interests), pre-register the runs, publish every generator, validator, seed and result file, and note that the benchmark's clearest failures---autocorrelation, and the floored strict-inequality $p$-value---apply to the author's own implementations as much as to anyone's.

\paragraph{Related work.}
\citet{arian2024backtest} compare out-of-sample testing schemes---walk-forward, $k$-fold, purged and combinatorial purged cross-validation---in a synthetic market with known structure, scoring them by the probability of backtest overfitting \citep{bailey2017pbo} and the deflated Sharpe ratio. Their object is the scheme that produces a backtest; ours is the correction applied to its result. \citet{santoni2026minerva} combine the deflated Sharpe ratio, the probability of backtest overfitting, the SPA test and the minimum track-record length into one robustness grade, and report its discrimination on synthetic markets and a pre-registered test on real ones; our results bear on its SPA component, whose size depends on how it is studentized and on the block length. \citet{harvey2020false} and \citet{chordia2020anomalies} study false and missed discoveries among many factors or anomalies, where the tests are applied across a cross-section rather than to the best of one search. \citet{hansen2005spa}, \citet{romano2005stepwise} and \citet{hsu2010stepwise} evaluate their tests by simulation in their own designs; Section~\ref{sec:grid} reconciles their results with ours. The size and power of multiple-testing procedures under dependence are well studied in statistics; our contribution is a small, open, versioned and pre-registered benchmark that uses the return shapes and search structures of trading research and calls each correction the way it is called in practice.
