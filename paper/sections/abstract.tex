\begin{abstract}
\noindent Corrections for backtest overfitting and data snooping---the deflated Sharpe ratio, the multiple-testing haircut, family-wise adjustments, White's Reality Check and Hansen's test for superior predictive ability (SPA)---are derived under assumptions that trading research violates. The Null Zoo is an open, versioned, pre-registered simulation benchmark that draws research searches (\nz{trials} strategies, \nz{obs} daily returns) from nine return families with known ground truth and scores \nz{v1.validator_count_no_oracle} validators and an oracle by size and power, with \nz{reps.v1} searches per cell. With a stationary bootstrap of mean block length 8, Hansen's studentized SPA test and the stepwise SPA test built on it rejected more than 6\% of skill-less searches at a nominal 5\% in at least \nz{both.fails6_min.spa_consistent} of nine families in each of two runs (up to \nz{both.size_max.spa_consistent}\%); the Reality Check exceeded 6\% only under autocorrelation. The bootstrap holds fixed a noisy, downward-biased long-run variance; a closed-form approximation, with proofs, shows that the resulting size exceeds the nominal level and grows with the block length, and accounts for about nine tenths of the excess. Re-studentizing every resample, as Romano and Wolf recommend, brought the size to about the Reality Check's (\nz{v2.A.size_range_nonar.spa_c_boot_t}\% outside the autocorrelated family). The released SPA test of the Python package arch does not studentize; the change merged in October 2026 adopts the fixed-variance version, which, run directly at its defaults, rejected \nz{archm.size.arch_default_consistent.iid_normal}\% of i.i.d.\ normal and \nz{archm.size.arch_default_consistent.skew_negative}\% of negatively skewed skill-less searches. On \nz{anom.k_min}--\nz{anom.k_max} published cross-sectional predictors in \nz{anom.windows} ten-year windows, the fixed-variance stepwise test declared \nz{anom.excess_fixed_pct}\% more predictors significant than a bootstrap-$t$, and \nz{anom.excess_q10_pct}\% more at arch's default block length. A lag-one autocorrelation of 0.2 raises the size of single-series tests that ignore it to about \nz{v1.size.luck_trials.ar1}\%, and the deflated Sharpe ratio's 0.95 rule misses \nz{v1.dsr_fail_to_accept.iid_normal}\% of i.i.d.\ searches containing a strategy with a true Sharpe ratio of 2. Of \nz{prereg.total_all} pre-registered predictions, \nz{prereg.held_all} held.
\end{abstract}
