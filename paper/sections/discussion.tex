\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Implications for practice.} Seven suggestions follow from the tables, within the limits below.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Re-studentize inside the bootstrap, or do not studentize.} A studentized data-snooping test whose resampled statistics reuse the sample's variance estimate is oversized with block resampling at two years of daily data, more so with long blocks, many strategies or negative skew. Re-studentizing every resample, as \citet{romano2005stepwise} recommend, kept the size near the Reality Check's at block length 8 and keeps the power that studentizing buys when the skilled strategy is among the least volatile.
  With independent strategies and block length 8 the Reality Check and the bootstrap-$t$ stayed within Monte Carlo error for 1 to 100 strategies, but every block-bootstrap test, the Reality Check included, was liberal with long blocks (block length 22) and up to 20 strategies, so the block length deserves as much care as the studentization. Under serial dependence the standard-deviation bootstrap-$t$ was liberal; re-studentizing by a natural block variance cured that case but was conservative elsewhere, so a block studentization that treats the observed and resampled statistics alike is still needed.
  \item \emph{Choose the ranking deliberately.} Studentizing, or ranking by $t$-statistic as LET does without resampling, finds a skilled low-volatility strategy that ranking by mean misses, and misses a skilled high-volatility strategy that ranking by mean finds.
  \item \emph{Recentre consistently and count ties.} Hansen's consistent recentring raises power at the nominal level when many variants are clearly poor; computing the $p$-value as $\#\{T^*\ge T\}/B$ stops a floored statistic from rejecting when no strategy beats the benchmark.
  The fix matters most for single-strategy calls and for searches with many poor variants.
  \item \emph{Check the selected strategy's returns for autocorrelation} before any multiple-testing adjustment. Lo's factor removes the excess in the autoregressive case it assumes. Kernel and block-bootstrap corrections reduce it without that assumption, but stay above the band (\nz{v1.size.hac_t_sidak.ar1}\% for HAC-$t$ and \nz{v1.size.bootstrap_t_sidak.ar1}\% for SB-$t$) and are liberal even under i.i.d.\ returns (\nz{v1.size.hac_t_sidak.iid_normal}\% and \nz{v1.size.bootstrap_t_sidak.iid_normal}\%).
  \item \emph{Compare corrections at matched sides and with the number of resamples stated.} Otherwise the comparison measures conventions: the haircut's conservatism comes from its two-sided convention, and a bootstrap's power from its number of resamples.
  \item \emph{Treat $\mathrm{DSR}\ge 0.95$ as the demanding rule it is.} It asks whether the best strategy beats the expected best of $N$ skill-less ones, not whether it has any skill, and over two years of daily data it passes few strategies with a true Sharpe ratio of 2.
  \item \emph{When returns are markedly skewed or fat-tailed, do not trust a single-series correction}: none in the benchmark is calibrated across negative skew, positive skew and fat tails, in part because selection itself distorts the moments of the chosen series. The Reality Check and the standard-deviation bootstrap-$t$, which resample all strategies together, stayed close to their level in those families.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.} The nine families change one feature at a time; real returns combine several, and the only serial dependence studied is AR(1), the Lo factor's best case. Every null hypothesis is the least favourable configuration, in which all means are zero, except in the poor-alternative designs. The main grid covers one search shape ($N=\nz{trials}$, $T=\nz{obs}$) and one strongly skilled strategy; experiments B and E vary $T$, the number of strategies and the block length under one or two families only, and real searches often contain many weakly skilled strategies. Apart from experiments C and C2, which use i.i.d.\ normal returns, the strategies have equal unconditional variances. The joint tests use 1,000 resamples, and the block length of every resampling test is fixed by rule rather than chosen from the data \citep{politis2004automatic}. Romano and Wolf's StepM was not run as they specify it. The deflated Sharpe ratio is scored only at its 0.95 point, not as a probability. No external implementation of the studentized test was run. Finally, a benchmark designed by the author of one of the scored methods, whose runs also chose defaults for the software of the author's firm, cannot be neutral in the sense of \citet{bartos2025living}. We pre-register the runs and publish every generator, validator, seed and result file, but independent replication with independently chosen families would be more convincing than anything we can do ourselves.

\paragraph{Next versions.} The benchmark is versioned so that it can grow without rewriting its history. Natural additions are families that combine features (skewed and autocorrelated returns) or have non-AR dependence, alternatives with many weakly skilled strategies, data-driven block lengths, Romano and Wolf's StepM as specified, and an estimate of the effective number of trials for correlated searches.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The corrections practitioners use for backtest overfitting and data snooping keep their promises in the setting they were derived for and fail in predictable ways outside it. The most consequential failure found here is the most technical: with block resampling, Hansen's studentized SPA test, recommended over the Reality Check for its power, is oversized at the two-year horizon of strategy research, because its bootstrap reuses a noisy, downward-biased variance estimate; arch's implementation stays much closer to its level because it does not studentize. Both have simple partial remedies: re-studentizing every resample, as Romano and Wolf recommend, which kept the size near the Reality Check's at block length 8, and, for arch, not adopting a fixed-variance studentization. Neither remedy removes the second lesson of the joint tests: long blocks make every block-bootstrap test liberal, because the bootstrap's own variance is the same noisy estimate. Autocorrelation remains the most damaging violation for the single-series corrections, and several apparent differences between methods come from conventions---sides, thresholds, numbers of resamples, ties---rather than from the methods. An open, pre-registered benchmark with known ground truth makes such statements checkable, and anyone can rerun or extend it from the published code and seeds.
