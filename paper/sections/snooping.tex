\subsection{With block resampling, the fixed-variance SPA test is oversized}
\label{sec:spa-size}
Hansen's studentized test for superior predictive ability, as Section~\ref{sec:design} defines it---a Politis--Romano variance at the bootstrap's mean block length of 8, held fixed in the resamples---is oversized at two years of daily data and twenty strategies (Table~\ref{tab:size-joint}). Its consistent $p$-value rejected more than the pre-registered 6\% of skill-less searches at a nominal 5\% in \nz{v1.fails6.spa_consistent} of the 9 families in v1 and in \nz{v1b.fails6.spa_consistent} in v1b, and up to \nz{both.size_max.spa_consistent}\%. In v1 it rejected \nz{v1.size.spa_consistent.iid_normal}\% under i.i.d.\ normal returns, \nz{v1.size.spa_consistent.skew_negative}\% under negative skew and \nz{v1.size.spa_consistent.ar1}\% under autocorrelation. The upper $p$-value behaves almost identically, and the stepwise SPA test, whose first step uses the same statistic and recentring, matched the consistent SPA test to within \nz{both.stepm_spa_maxdiff} percentage points in every family of both runs. Romano and Wolf's StepM, which re-estimates the standard errors in every resample, was not run; the re-studentized variants below indicate how it would behave.

White's Reality Check, which uses the same bootstrap but does not studentize, rejected \nz{both.size_range_nonar.reality_check}\% of skill-less searches outside the AR(1) family in the two runs, and \nz{v1.size.reality_check.ar1}\% and \nz{v1b.size.reality_check.ar1}\% under autocorrelation. Outside the AR(1) family it lies above the 99\% band only in \nz{v1.above_band_nonar.reality_check} in v1 and \nz{v1b.above_band_nonar.reality_check} in v1b. Unlike any single-series correction, it stays close to its level under negative skew, positive skew, fat tails, volatility clustering and correlated strategies at once.

This was not the comparison v1 was designed to make. Its pre-registration listed the two SPA $p$-values as candidates for the author's software and only measured the Reality Check, so the pre-registered rule chose the consistent SPA test, which failed the size bar in most families. Rather than switch to the Reality Check on that evidence, a second pre-registration (v1b) added it as the first candidate and repeated the run on fresh seeds. v1b confirmed the pattern; its rule chose the Reality Check, which missed the bar only in \nz{v1b.headline.joint.fails}. In v2, the second implementation reproduced v1b's sizes of the consistent SPA test and the Reality Check in all nine families to within three Monte Carlo standard errors (prediction P1 \nz{v2.P1.held}; the largest difference was \nz{v2.P1.maxdiff} points).

\subsection{Why: a variance estimate the bootstrap holds fixed}
\label{sec:why}
The studentized statistic divides each mean by $\hat\omega_k$, estimated once from the sample, and the resampled statistics divide by the same $\hat\omega_k$. The bootstrap therefore reproduces the sampling variation of the means but not that of the estimate in the denominator. \citet[footnote~22]{romano2005stepwise} note that this choice is only first-order consistent and that it is preferable to compute the standard errors from the bootstrap data \citep{hall1992bootstrap}, as their StepM does; \citet{hall1991guidelines} state the general guideline. What follows measures the size cost of not doing so at the sample lengths, block lengths and numbers of strategies of strategy research.

The size cost has a simple form under i.i.d.\ normal returns. For one series $x_1,\dots,x_T$ with sample mean $\bar x$, let $\hat\gamma_i=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T-i}(x_t-\bar x)(x_{t+i}-\bar x)$ and let $\hat\omega^2=\hat\gamma_0+2\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}\kappa_i\hat\gamma_i$ be the Politis--Romano estimate, with weights $\kappa_i=(1-i/T)(1-p)^i+(i/T)(1-p)^{T-i}$ and $p=1/q$ for mean block length $q$. Define
\begin{equation}
r_T(q)=\frac{T-1}{T}-\frac{2}{T^2}\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}\kappa_i\,(T-i),\qquad v_T(q)=\frac{2}{T}\Bigl(1+\frac{2}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}\kappa_i^2\,(T-i)\Bigr).
\label{eq:rv}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Lemma 1.} \emph{(a) If $x_1,\dots,x_T$ are i.i.d.\ with variance $\sigma^2$, then $E[\hat\omega^2]=\sigma^2 r_T(q)$. (b) If they are also normal, $\bar x$ is independent of $\hat\omega^2$, and $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\omega^2)=\sigma^4 v_T(q)+O(T^{-2})$. (c) Both $1-r_T(q)$ and $v_T(q)$ increase with $q$.}

\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1.} \emph{Let $N$ strategies have independent i.i.d.\ normal excess returns with mean zero, and let the fixed-variance test reject when $\max_k\sqrt T\,\bar x_k/\hat\omega_k$ exceeds $z^*=\Phi^{-1}\bigl((1-\alpha)^{1/N}\bigr)$, the first-order limit of its bootstrap critical value. If $\hat\omega_k^2/(\sigma_k^2 r_T)$ is approximated by $\chi^2_\nu/\nu$ with $\nu=2/v_T$, its rejection probability is}
\begin{equation}
\pi_T(q)=1-\Bigl[1-\bar F_\nu\bigl(z^*\sqrt{r_T(q)}\bigr)\Bigr]^N,
\label{eq:size}
\end{equation}
\emph{where $\bar F_\nu$ is the upper tail of Student's $t$ with $\nu$ degrees of freedom. Then $\pi_T(q)>\alpha$ for every $q$ and $T$, $\pi_T(q)$ increases with $q$, and $\pi_T(q)\to\alpha$ as $T\to\infty$ with $q$ fixed.}

The proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The bootstrap cannot correct either term. Under the stationary bootstrap the conditional variance of $\sqrt T$ times a resampled mean is the Politis--Romano estimate itself \citep[Lemma~1]{politis1994stationary}, so a resample divided by the same $\hat\omega_k$ has unit variance whatever the error in $\hat\omega_k$, and its critical value stays near $z^*$. At $T=\nz{obs}$, $\hat\omega$ has a coefficient of variation of \nz{approx.cv_w.b8}\% at block length 8 and \nz{approx.cv_w.b22}\% at 22, against \nz{approx.cv_w.b1}\% for the sample standard deviation, so $\nu\approx\nz{approx.nu.b8}$ and \nz{approx.nu.b22}; $\hat\omega^2$ is biased downward by \nz{approx.bias.b8}\% and \nz{approx.bias.b22}\%; and with $z^*=\nz{approx.z_star}$ for \nz{trials} strategies, \eqref{eq:size} gives \nz{approx.size.b8}\% and \nz{approx.size.b22}\%, against \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c.iid_normal}\% and \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_b22.iid_normal}\% measured in experiment A: \nz{approx.share.b8}\% and \nz{approx.share.b22}\% of the excess. Without the bias term ($r_T=1$) it would give only \nz{approx.size_nobias.b8}\% and \nz{approx.size_nobias.b22}\%. The formula ignores the variability of the bootstrap's own critical value, which Appendix~\ref{sec:inherit} measures. With block length 1, the setting of Hansen's own simulations, the Politis--Romano variance is the sample variance, and \eqref{eq:size} gives almost no excess (\nz{approx.size.b1}\%). A bootstrap-$t$, which re-estimates the standard error in every resample, reproduces the variability of the denominator and removes the first-order term \citep{hall1992bootstrap}.

Experiment A tests the account directly (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}). Studentizing by the sample standard deviation instead, with the bootstrap unchanged, removes most of the excess in the families with symmetric, serially uncorrelated and independent strategies: \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_sd.iid_normal}\% under i.i.d.\ normal returns, \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_sd.student_t4}\% under $t_4$, \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_sd.garch}\% under GARCH and \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_sd.regimes}\% with volatility regimes. With correlated strategies it removes part of the excess (\nz{v2.A.size.spa_c.correlated_trials}\% to \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_sd.correlated_trials}\%, and \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c.block_cluster}\% to \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_sd.block_cluster}\% in the cluster family). Under negative skew it removes little (\nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_sd.skew_negative}\%), because a second effect appears: a high sample mean tends to come with a low sample standard deviation, so the studentized statistic has a heavier upper tail than any fixed denominator can reproduce. A bootstrap-$t$ that re-studentizes every resample by its own standard deviation reproduces both effects, in line with Hall and Wilson's guideline. Under negative skew its size was \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_boot_t.skew_negative}\%, and outside the AR(1) family \nz{v2.A.size_range_nonar.spa_c_boot_t}\%, about the Reality Check's (\nz{v2.A.size_range_nonar.rc}\%) rather than calibrated in every family: both are above the band in the cluster family (prediction P3 \nz{v2.P3.held}, P4 \nz{v2.P4.held}). With the long block that arch uses by default ($\lfloor\sqrt{T}\rfloor = 22$), the fixed-variance test rejected \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_b22.iid_normal}\% of i.i.d.\ normal searches and \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_b22.skew_negative}\% under negative skew (P6 \nz{v2.P6.held}).

The mechanism was suggested by an unregistered pilot (four families, 2,000 searches each, disclosed with its results in the v2 pre-registration) and confirmed in the registered run; v2 is a confirmation on fresh seeds, not a test of a hypothesis formed before any data were seen.

\subsection{How the excess depends on the sample and the search}
\label{sec:grid}
The excess of the fixed-variance test is a finite-sample effect: it shrinks as the sample grows and grows with the number of strategies (Table~\ref{tab:grid}; prediction P7 \nz{v2.P7.held}). Under i.i.d.\ normal returns it rejected \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.iid_normal.n252_k20}\% of skill-less searches with one year of daily data, \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.iid_normal.n504_k20}\% with two and \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.iid_normal.n2520_k20}\% with ten; with two years of data it rejected \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.iid_normal.n504_k5}\% of searches of five strategies and \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.iid_normal.n504_k100}\% of searches of a hundred. Under negative skew the same figures were \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.skew_negative.n252_k20}\%, \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.skew_negative.n504_k20}\%, \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.skew_negative.n2520_k20}\%, \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.skew_negative.n504_k5}\% and \nz{v2.B.size.spa_c.skew_negative.n504_k100}\%. The bootstrap-$t$ and the Reality Check stayed closer to 5\% (\nz{v2.B.range.spa_c_boot_t}\% and \nz{v2.B.range.rc}\% over the \nz{v2.B.cells} cells) but not within the band everywhere: the bootstrap-$t$ is above it in \nz{v2.B.above_band.spa_c_boot_t} cells and the Reality Check outside it in \nz{v2.B.outside_band.rc}.

Earlier simulations did not report this excess, for identifiable reasons. \citet{hansen2005spa} used $q=1$, i.i.d.\ resampling, ``in accordance with the lack of time-dependence'' in his designs (p.~22 of the working-paper version); the Politis--Romano variance is then the sample variance, which is our SPA$_c$-SD column, close to nominal under i.i.d.\ returns. The excess appears only with block resampling, which dependent returns require and which arch uses by default. The working-paper version of \citet{hsu2010stepwise} reports family-wise error rates of the studentized stepwise SPA test of 3.2\%, 1.8\% and 1.2\% with 90, 900 and 9,000 neutral strategies and 1,000 periods, from 500 replications (a standard error of about one point) with 500 resamples; their weight function, as printed, gives the $j$-th autocovariance the weight $(1-Q)^j$ with $Q=0.9$, which, if computed that way, makes their studentizer almost the sample standard deviation. \citet{romano2005stepwise} found satisfactory control of the family-wise error for their re-studentized StepM with i.i.d.\ data, 100 periods, 40 strategies and Efron's bootstrap. We have not reproduced these designs. The general problem---tests studentized by a noisy long-run variance are oversized in finite samples---is well known in heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-robust inference \citep{kiefer2005new,sun2008optimal,lazarus2018har}, including tests of equal predictive accuracy \citep{coroneo2020comparing}, and \citet{goncalves2011block} show that a block bootstrap which recomputes the long-run variance in each resample with the formula used on the data reproduces the fixed-$b$ approximation and can be much more accurate than the normal one. Distinct from this finite-sample effect, \citet{frederiksen2026heavy} show that the Diebold--Mariano test can be severely oversized even asymptotically when loss differentials have infinite variance.

\subsection{The bootstrap's own variance and long blocks}
Every block-bootstrap test, studentized or not, inherits the noisy, downward-biased Politis--Romano estimate through its critical value. Experiment E (Appendix~\ref{sec:inherit}) shows the effect is small at block length 8 but makes the Reality Check and the bootstrap-$t$ liberal at block length 22 with up to 20 strategies (\nz{v2b.E.range_k20minus.rc.b22}\% and \nz{v2b.E.range_k20minus.spa_c_boot_t_ge.b22}\%), while the fixed-variance test compounds the two effects (\nz{v2b.E.size.spa_c_ge.k20.b8}\% with twenty strategies at block length 8). Re-studentizing each resample by its natural block variance (experiment F, Appendix~\ref{sec:block-student}) cures the bootstrap-$t$'s excess under autocorrelation but is conservative elsewhere.

\subsection{What this means for software}
\label{sec:software}
The open-source Python package arch \citep{sheppard2025arch} does not studentize its SPA statistic. In version \nz{v2.D.arch}, \texttt{SPA.compute()} and the routine that simulates the resampled statistics never read the \texttt{studentize} attribute; the option only sets a label in the test's information, and the variance estimate enters only the consistent recentring threshold. Experiment D confirms it: on \nz{v2.D.total_pairs} search-and-block-length pairs, arch's $p$-values were identical with \texttt{studentize=True} and \texttt{studentize=False} on every pair, and agreed with this paper's unstudentized consistent test to within Monte Carlo error (mean absolute difference \nz{v2.D.meanabs_range}, against \nz{v2.D.meanabs_expected} expected from two independent bootstraps of \nz{v2.reps} resamples; Table~\ref{tab:arch}). These statements concern \texttt{SPA} with \texttt{nested=False}. Arch's SPA test is thus the Reality Check with Hansen's consistent recentring. It is much less oversized than the fixed-variance test, but not calibrated at its default block length of 22: our implementation of the same computation rejected \nz{v2.A.size_range_nonar.spa_c_unstud_b22}\% of skill-less searches outside the AR(1) family at block length 22 (\nz{v2.A.size_range_nonar.spa_c_unstud}\% at block length 8), and arch itself rejected \nz{v2.D.size.arch_default_consistent.iid_normal}\% of i.i.d.\ normal searches at its default in experiment D. Prediction P5, which \nz{v2.P5.held}, was registered and scored at block length 8 only.

A pull request opened on 27 September 2026 and merged on 6 October 2026 divides both the statistic and the resampled values by the fixed standard error $\sqrt{\hat\omega^2/T}$, in SPA and in StepM, whenever \texttt{studentize} is true, which is the default \citep{arch871}. At arch's default block length and apart from the floor at zero, that is the computation measured above at \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_b22.iid_normal}\% and \nz{v2.A.size.spa_c_b22.skew_negative}\%. In an unregistered check we then ran the merged code itself (arch \nz{archm.version}, merge commit \nz{archm.commit}) on \nz{archm.searches} new null searches per family. At its default block length it rejected \nz{archm.size.arch_default_consistent.iid_normal}\% of i.i.d.\ normal, \nz{archm.size.arch_default_consistent.skew_negative}\% of negatively skewed and \nz{archm.size.arch_default_consistent.ar1}\% of AR(1) searches, against \nz{archm.size.ours_default_spa_c.iid_normal}\%, \nz{archm.size.ours_default_spa_c.skew_negative}\% and \nz{archm.size.ours_default_spa_c.ar1}\% for our implementation of the fixed-variance test on the same searches; at block length 8 the figures were \nz{archm.size.arch_b8_consistent.iid_normal}\%, \nz{archm.size.arch_b8_consistent.skew_negative}\% and \nz{archm.size.arch_b8_consistent.ar1}\%. Released arch \nz{v2.D.arch}, on the searches of experiment D, rejected \nz{v2.D.size.arch_default_consistent.iid_normal}\%, \nz{v2.D.size.arch_default_consistent.skew_negative}\% and \nz{v2.D.size.arch_default_consistent.ar1}\%. Re-studentizing every resample would avoid most of the excess; we reported these figures to the maintainers before the change was released \citep{arch879}.

A second, smaller issue concerns ties. With the statistic floored at zero, $T^{\mathrm{SPA}}=0$ whenever no strategy beats the benchmark in sample. Strategies below the consistent threshold are recentred at zero rather than at their means, so if every strategy falls below it, the floored resampled statistics are mostly zero as well, and a $p$-value with a strict inequality, $\#\{T^*>T\}/B$---the definition in \citet{hansen2005spa} (p.~20 of the working-paper version)---counts only the few positive resamples: the test rejects although no strategy did better than the benchmark. With a single strategy whose mean is exactly zero this happens with probability $\Phi(-\sqrt{2\ln\ln T})=\nz{ties.p_below.k1}\%$, and in experiment E the strict-inequality version of the fixed-variance test had size \nz{v2b.E.size.spa_c_strict.k1.b8}\% with one strategy at block length 8, against \nz{v2b.E.size.spa_c_ge.k1.b8}\% with ties counted. In v2 the defect caused the second failed prediction: P9 expected the bootstrap-$t$ version to keep its size in a design whose other strategies were clearly poor, and it rejected \nz{v2.C.size.spa_c_boot_t.poor_alternatives}\% (Table~\ref{tab:fair}). An exploratory check after that run, on a fresh seed, traced the excess to the ties (searches with $T=0$ contributed \nz{explore.size_T_zero.spa_c_boot_t_strict} points of the bootstrap-$t$ test's \nz{explore.size.spa_c_boot_t_strict}\%), and v2b registered the fix. Counting ties, $p=\#\{T^*\ge T\}/B$, which gives $p=1$ whenever $T=0$, the floored tests' size in that design was \nz{v2b.C2.size.spa_c_ge.poor}\% and \nz{v2b.C2.size.spa_c_boot_t_ge.poor}\%, against \nz{v2b.C2.size.spa_c_strict.poor}\% with the strict inequality (P16 \nz{v2b.P16.held}). The implementation behind v1, the author's, also uses the strict inequality. Under v1's null hypotheses, where every strategy has mean zero, the case is too rare to matter (at most \nz{ties.p_all_below.max_per_100000} searches in 100,000 under a normal approximation), but it matters for single-strategy calls and for searches with many poor variants.

\subsection{Where studentizing and recentring pay off}
Studentizing pays when strategies' volatilities differ and the skilled strategy is among the less volatile, because it ranks by $t$-statistic; consistent recentring pays when the other strategies are clearly poor, provided ties are counted. Experiment C2 quantifies both (Appendix~\ref{app:pay}).

\subsection{Power}
Under i.i.d.\ normal and correlated returns, studentizing buys no power: at a Sharpe ratio of 2 the bootstrap-$t$ version and the Reality Check had similar size-adjusted power (\nz{v2.A.adj.spa_c_boot_t.iid_normal}\% and \nz{v2.A.adj.rc.iid_normal}\% under i.i.d.\ normal returns, \nz{v2.A.adj.spa_c_boot_t.correlated_trials}\% and \nz{v2.A.adj.rc.correlated_trials}\% in the correlated family; Table~\ref{tab:mechanism-power}). Where realized volatilities differ across strategies even though their unconditional variances are equal, the bootstrap-$t$ gains a few points (\nz{v2.A.adj.spa_c_boot_t.garch}\% against \nz{v2.A.adj.rc.garch}\% under GARCH, \nz{v2.A.adj.spa_c_boot_t.regimes}\% against \nz{v2.A.adj.rc.regimes}\% with volatility regimes, \nz{v2.A.adj.spa_c_boot_t.student_t4}\% against \nz{v2.A.adj.rc.student_t4}\% under $t_4$). Skew pulls the two apart in opposite directions: under negative skew the Reality Check was ahead (\nz{v2.A.adj.rc.skew_negative}\% against \nz{v2.A.adj.spa_c_boot_t.skew_negative}\%), under positive skew the bootstrap-$t$ (\nz{v2.A.adj.spa_c_boot_t.skew_positive}\% against \nz{v2.A.adj.rc.skew_positive}\%). The fixed-variance test's apparent power advantage (raw \nz{v1.power2.spa_consistent.iid_normal}\% in v1, against \nz{v1.power2.reality_check.iid_normal}\% for the Reality Check) disappears once its excess size is removed (\nz{v1.adj2.spa_consistent.iid_normal}\% against \nz{v1.adj2.reality_check.iid_normal}\%).

\subsection{The pre-registered predictions}
Of the \nz{v2.predictions.count} predictions registered before the v2 run, \nz{v2.predictions.held_count} held; of the \nz{v2b.predictions.count} registered before v2b, \nz{v2b.predictions.held_count} held (Tables~\ref{tab:predictions} and~\ref{tab:predictions-v2b}). The three that failed are reported above: P2 and P9 in v2, and P12 in v2b. The explanation of P9 came from an exploratory check made after the v2 run; v2b then tested it as a registered prediction (P16).
